Your Next Chapter: Recognising and Escaping Financial Abuse

By Melissa White, Mortgage Broker & Finance Specialist, MoneyQuest Broadbeach

Financial abuse is a pattern of control where someone limits or takes away your access to money, manipulates your financial decisions, or uses your money without your consent. It can come from a partner, a family member or anyone else with access to you or your funds.

It often goes unrecognised because it happens gradually. If something in this guide feels familiar, you're not imagining it, and support is available.

What financial abuse can look like

  • Your partner controls your income and bank accounts.
  • You're discouraged, shamed or threatened when you use money or make purchases.
  • You're kept in the dark about finances, and decisions that affect you aren't discussed.
  • Debt is built up in your name without your knowledge or agreement.

It's recognised under the law

Under Queensland law, economically abusive behaviour is a form of domestic violence. Since 26 May 2025, coercive control, a pattern of abusive behaviour that can include financial control, has been a criminal offence in Queensland.

There are ways to rebuild

  1. 1
    Acknowledge and heal. Recognise what you're going through; it's real and valid. Support groups, services and people you trust can help. Take time to heal and put yourself first.
  2. 2
    Build your security and safety plan. Open a bank account in your name and start depositing income or savings into it, so you can build an escape fund. Change your email address to one only you can access, and keep important documents somewhere safe that you can always get to.
  3. 3
    Learn finance essentials. Learn the basics: income, expenses, budgeting with purpose, saving consistently and investing in your future.
  4. 4
    Create a plan for financial freedom. Set short- and long-term goals, build an emergency fund and plan for independence.
  5. 5
    Grow your financial confidence. Take steps to grow your income, investments and credit history in your own name. You're in control, and you can build the life you deserve.

Check your credit file

If you suspect debt has been taken out in your name, request your free credit report from Equifax, illion or Experian. Our guide Rebuilding Your Credit Score explains how to dispute errors and start rebuilding.

Remember, you're healing, you're becoming stronger, you're not alone.
