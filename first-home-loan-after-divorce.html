Your First Home Loan: What Changes After Divorce or Separation

By Melissa White, Mortgage Broker & Finance Specialist, MoneyQuest Broadbeach

When you apply for a home loan as an individual after separation, the picture a lender sees changes. How much you can borrow, the documents you provide and the lenders that suit you can all be different from the last time you applied as a couple.

You're far from the only one going through this. The steps below help you understand what lenders look at and how to prepare before you apply.

What lenders look at differently

  • Serviceability on one income. The lender works out whether your income alone covers the repayments plus your living costs.
  • Joint debts still in your name. A joint loan or card counts against you in full until it's refinanced, paid out or closed.
  • Your credit history. Late payments on joint accounts during the separation can show on your report.
  • Your deposit or equity position. Funds from a property settlement may form part of your deposit.
  • Ongoing living costs. Child support paid or received, school fees and childcare are all factored in.

A common misconception

Many people assume a lender will look at what they could borrow as a couple. They don't. Serviceability is recalculated from scratch on your current individual position.

Steps before you apply

  • List every debt, including anything still held jointly.
  • Work out your borrowing capacity on your income alone.
  • Check your credit report for errors or surprises.
  • Gather updated income documents.
  • Decide whether you'll apply solo, with a guarantor or with a new co-borrower.

Buying before the joint home has sold?

If you're selling your joint home and using your share of the sale funds to buy your next one, the timing doesn't always line up. Bridging finance can cover the gap between buying your new home and the sale of the existing one settling, so you don't miss the right property while you wait.

How we can help

  • Identify lenders comfortable with post-separation circumstances.
  • Arrange bridging finance if you're buying before the joint home sells.
  • Help you access any government grants and incentives you may be eligible for, at no cost.
